# Trane look mechanics

Trane is a softly furred golden retriever with a separate head and neck, physical brown eyes, floppy ears, a red collar and a plain round tag. Natural looking means eyes lead, the whole head follows with restrained neck yaw and pitch, and ears follow the head while hanging under gravity. Preserve his skull, muzzle length, eye spacing and warm expression. Rotate/redraw eyeballs, eyelids and visible sclera together inside their sockets; do not overlay new eyes or slide loose pupils.

Anchor the seated haunches, planted front paws, lower chest and compact tail. Collar remains around the fixed neck base and tag rests at the upper chest. Body faces the viewer throughout; no whole-sprite rotation, body rocking, torso yaw or tail switching sides. Only subtle upper-neck fur follows the turn. Keep the same practical body size and baseline as idle/00.

Cardinal families in viewer/screen coordinates:
- 000 up: head tips back on neck, nose aimed upward above face center, chin/underside muzzle visible, forehead foreshortened, whole eyes looking up. No lateral turn.
- 090 screen-right: head yaws clearly right, nose tip well to screen-right of head center, rightward eye gaze, muzzle projects toward right edge. Screen-left cheek/ear surface becomes broad; far-side facial features partly occluded. Head stays level vertically.
- 180 down: head pitches forward/down, nose below face center aimed toward front paws, crown more visible, eyes/lids lowered with visible downward attention. No lateral turn.
- 270 screen-left: head yaws clearly left, nose tip well to screen-left of head center, leftward eye gaze, muzzle projects toward left edge. Screen-right cheek/ear surface becomes broad; opposite features partly occluded. Head stays level vertically.

Motion budget: a smooth clockwise ellipse of head attention around the anchored torso. From 000 through 090 to 180, each 22.5-degree step changes yaw/pitch by a similar visual amount. Row 10 continues one step after 157.5, passes 270, and ends 337.5 one step before 000. Diagonals combine the correct vertical pitch and horizontal yaw; near-vertical intermediates still need a small visible lateral cue. No single adjacent pair snaps, changes head volume, relocates a paw, flips the tag or changes fur style. Every gaze differs visibly from neutral. Keep ears attached, collar fixed and all bodies complete with padding.
